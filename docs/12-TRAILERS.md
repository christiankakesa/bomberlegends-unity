### ChatGPT

```md
Yes. I would structure the trailer around **one very simple promise**:

> **You are a Neon Soul. You fight through corrupted sectors by turning bombs into your own fighting style, growing stronger, and unleashing absurd neon explosions to liberate the city.**

The important thing is that the trailer should **show the game before explaining the lore**. The player should understand the genre and core loop in the first 10–15 seconds.

## Recommended trailer structure — ~75–90 seconds

### 0:00–0:05 — Establish the genre immediately

**Goal:** The viewer must understand *“this is a bomber/action game”* almost instantly.

Visual:

* Black screen.
* A tiny neon character enters a grid-based dungeon.
* A bomb is thrown/dropped.
* The character dodges around a corner.
* **BOOM.**
* The explosion destroys a chain of blocks/enemies.
* Camera rapidly pulls back to reveal the full arena.

On-screen text:

> **NEON ACTION BOMBER**

Then immediately:

> **MASTER YOUR BOMB ART**

Sound:

* Short electronic heartbeat.
* Bomb fuse.
* Explosion hits exactly on the music drop.

Don't start with the lore. **Start with the bomb.**

---

# 0:05–0:18 — The Hook

Now show why *Bomber Legends* isn't just another Bomberman-like game.

We need to communicate:

**Bomb → movement → destruction → combat → style.**

Example sequence:

1. Player drops a bomb.
2. Runs through a corridor.
3. Bomb explodes.
4. Explosion kills several enemies.
5. Explosion chains into another bomb.
6. Blocks disappear.
7. Player picks up an upgrade.
8. Suddenly the next bomb is enormous.
9. Player uses it to destroy a miniboss.
10. Quick montage of different Bomb Arts.

The camera should become increasingly spectacular.

Text/VO:

> **DROP. DODGE. DESTROY.**

Then:

> **BUT YOUR BOMBS ARE YOUR WEAPON.**

Then showcase 3–4 Bomb Arts extremely quickly.

For example:

* giant cross explosion
* bouncing bomb
* homing bomb
* drilling bomb
* chain reaction
* elemental/neon bomb
* teleport bomb

The viewer should think:

**“Oh. I don't just place bombs. I build a fighting style around them.”**

That's the core hook.

---

# 0:18–0:32 — Show the actual game loop

Now make the game extremely understandable.

Don't explain systems with UI panels. **Show the loop.**

### Sequence

**ENTER SECTOR**

**GET REWARDS**

↓

Choose an upgrade

↓

**BUILD YOUR BOMB ART**

↓

Fight stronger enemies

↓

**SENTINEL LORD**

↓

↓

**LIBERATE SECTOR**

The trailer can literally use very short text cards:

> **ENTER**

> **FIGHT**

> **BUILD**

> **MASTER**

> **LIBERATE**

This gives the viewer a mental model of the game.

> **OUR WILL.**

Now the trailer becomes much more emotional.

This is where **Neon Soul** becomes meaningful.

# 0:50–1:05 — Escalation / Awakening

Now go completely crazy.

Show that the game isn't only about destroying blocks.

The player:

* fights a Sentinel Lord
* gets surrounded
* uses Bomb Art
* destroys the arena
* collects Fractured Hearts
* unlocks an upgrade
* changes bomb behavior
* uses the environment

HUD:

Silence.

**AWAKENING.**

But importantly:

Buildings light up.

People regain color.

The city literally comes back to life.

This makes the fantasy of the game very clear:

# The key cinematic rhythm

I would deliberately make the trailer follow this progression:

**0–15 sec**

### "What is this?"

**15–30 sec**

### "Why is it fun?"

**30–50 sec**

### "Why does it matter?"

**50–65 sec**

### "Holy shit."

**65–75 sec**

### "I want to know what happens."

**75–90 sec**

### "I want to be this character."

That's much stronger than trying to explain the entire lore.

---

## One important change I'd make to the lore presentation

I would **not** introduce:

> "In the megacity Ébène-Prime, color itself has been stolen..."

as narration at the beginning.

It's good lore, but it's not good **opening trailer communication**.

Instead, **show it**.

Start with a vibrant Neon Soul fighting.

Then suddenly cut to:

**A completely grey city.**

No music.

No color.

People frozen.

A child's memory disappearing.

Then the Neon Soul walks into the darkness.

Their bomb starts glowing.

**BOOM.**

Color returns.

Now the audience understands the lore emotionally without needing a paragraph of exposition.

---

# The trailer's central visual metaphor

I think this could become the identity of **Bomber Legends**:

### 💜 Corruption = silence, grey, static, darkness

### 💥 Neon Souls = explosions, music, color, emotion

### 🌈 Liberation = the world coming back to life

So every time the player gets stronger, the **visual world becomes more alive**.

That gives the trailer a very satisfying visual progression:

**Grey → Purple → Neon → Explosion → Full Color**

And it perfectly connects the **bombing gameplay** with the **story**.

The player isn't just blowing things up.

**They're literally exploding color back into the world.**

That is, in my opinion, the strongest hook in your lore.
```
